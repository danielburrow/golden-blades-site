This page is built from the Blades' own advice in #kit-and-gear (March 2024 to November 2026). Prices are what Blades reported at the time and will drift, so check the shop. A shop appearing here means Blades have used or recommended it, not that we guarantee it. Not sure about something? Ask in the Blades Discord. Someone has almost certainly solved it already.

Before You Buy

1. Ask about a House order

Several times a year the House pools orders with distributor pricing. Cheaper, better shipping, and delivered at meetups.

2. Think about weapons check

Not every shop's weapons pass. Ask before you buy a weapon or shield.

3. Count the real cost

Shipping, import fees, and sizing mistakes have caught plenty of Blades. Details below.

How House orders work

  • The House holds distributor accounts with several suppliers: Atelier Nemesis, B3, Burgschneider, Calimacil, Epic Armoury, Leonardo Carbone, LARP Distribution, Mytholon, and others. Orders are batched, announced in the Discord with a "last call", and delivered in person at meetups.
  • To get in, build your cart on the shop's site, screenshot it, and send it to the person running the order. Ask in #kit-and-gear who that is right now.
  • Some shops have minimums or heavy shipping on solo orders (Epic Armoury shipping on a non-shield order was about $34 in 2024), so batching saves real money.
  • Lead times vary. Calimacil is slow to ship, so order well before a big event. Blades have also negotiated group discounts with individual makers (for example Atemporalia).
  • Some Blades also buy through Medieval Collectibles with a House discount. Ask before you check out.

Weapons check

  • Blades report that Atelier Nemesis, Calimacil, and Epic Armoury weapons usually pass. Mytholon weapons rarely do.
  • Epic results are mixed. Blades have reported newer Epic items having trouble, and at least one Epic sword passed but was not approved for thrusting. Nobody keeps a master list, so ask before you order.
  • Fakesteel weapons are built for slashing. They are not approved for thrusting, which matters for a longsword.
  • Homemade weapons will not pass weapons check at Hynafol.
  • Shields: bring yours to weapons check. Expect staff to look for about 2 inches of padding on exposed edges.

Shipping, tariffs, and sizing

  • Orders from outside the US can pick up tariffs and import fees on top of shipping. Blades have paid anywhere from around $15 to $100 in fees on a single order, and shipping on some European clothing costs more than the clothing.
  • Crossbows and crossbow parts from Europe have been held up in customs. Order them through a US distributor when you can.
  • Email the seller about sizing before you order. One Blade got an answer from a tights maker within 20 minutes, and custom makers are usually happy to confirm measurements. Check whether a "height" field means height or inseam.
  • Cheap plate armor often runs big. Plan on extra padding under it.

Cheap marketplace listings

  • Be careful with very cheap armor and clothing on marketplaces. Some sellers advertise with photos borrowed from better makers. Buy only when you see several reviews with customer photos, and do not make a suspiciously cheap listing your first purchase.
  • If a custom, puncture-rated item costs far less than anyone else's, ask why.

Getting the colors right

Blue and yellow is surprisingly hard to find off the shelf. Blades solve it three ways: buy shops that sell blue and gold directly (Burgschneider and Verillas), buy natural or undyed pieces and dye them (Apparatus LARP sells fabric that takes dye well), or order custom. See Kit & Colors in the Primer for what is expected.

Armor & Protection

"What do I buy first?"

Blades who fight with two-handed weapons say gauntlets and forearm protection give the biggest jump in survivability, followed by a helmet. A common start is soft leather work gloves with the logo cut off, plus Epic Armoury's arm and leg bracer and greave bundle. After that, add pauldrons, then chest protection.

A rudimentary padded starter kit that circulated in August 2024 used these sticker prices, so expect to pay somewhat less on a House order:

"Where do I get a breastplate?"

  • Arthammer. A full set (breastplate, backplate, and tassets) cost about $250 for one Blade in late 2025. A couple of Blades have used them.
  • Burgschneider. Retail was about $190 in 2024. A popular, well-liked option.
  • Epic Armoury. The cheapest. The Soldier Torso was around $40 and the RFB around $50 in 2024. Stock comes and goes.
  • Medieval Collectibles. Carries the Pikeman Cuirass by Marshall Historical, which one Blade called hard to beat on value with a House discount. Irongate Armory in Finland has stocked the same style when it was sold out. Mytholon plate is also sold here, around $70 in 2024.
  • WeDieWithStyle. Pricier custom option that Blades report holds up well.

"Where do I get a helmet?"

  • Burgschneider for a kettle helm.
  • Epic Armoury for the "secret helmet", a helmet you can wear a hat over. Show the weapons check staff it is on underneath.
  • Medieval Collectibles and Kult of Athena for more helmet styles.
  • Wholesale Blades for very cheap helmets. One Blade bought several and calls the shop legit. Treat them as budget gear and inspect them when they arrive.
  • Helmets usually run big. A padded arming cap or Amazon helmet liner pads fix most fit problems.

"Where do I get chainmail?"

Ringmesh is the favorite among Blades who have bought mail. Choose your options at purchase, including black. It costs more and takes a few extra weeks. Medieval Collectibles sells aluminum aventails and cheaper mail pieces.

"Where do I get a gambeson?"

  • Burgschneider for the standard short-sleeved gambeson.
  • El Marques on Etsy for fancier styles. Shipping cost a lot on small orders until the seller adjusted it, so message them first.
  • Medieval Collectibles for the Adam gambeson.
  • Renaissance by RC on Etsy for custom-fit gambesons in your colors and size. One Blade came back for three blue and yellow ones.

Budget & Starter Finds

  • Amazon is the go-to for cheap starter gear: knee-high or soccer socks, scarves in House colors, helmet liner pads, cord and ties, and bulk basics. Bulk Amazon buys are often the best value per dollar.
  • Goodwill and thrift stores are good for practice fabric. Linen tablecloths, curtains, and bed sheets work for prototypes and pattern fitting. Wash them first.
  • Home Depot and Harbor Freight are good for work gloves, welding gloves, and drop cloths for camp.
  • Leonardo Carbone sells starter clothing, and the House gets good prices through group orders.
  • Spirit Halloween is a handy last-minute source for accessories.
  • Zootzu makes Texas-weight garb that looks good.

Borrowing is also fine. Companies lend gear to new recruits. See Kit & Colors.

Camp & Decorum

  • Camp gear. LARP Distribution sells decorum-friendly cutlery, plates, and decorative items, and the House orders from them.
  • Chairs. Chivari sells slatted wood folding chairs in sets of four.
  • Table covers and drop cloths. Harbor Freight drop cloths are a cheap fix.
  • Tent cleaning. One Blade heard a vinegar solution works on canvas.
  • Tents. Epic Armoury tents are the common pick, and you can buy replacement poles and panels instead of a whole new tent. Their tents sell out often. Tentsmiths makes higher-end canvas tents that meet reenactor standards. Check with the event's decorum rules before buying anything with modern fabric.

Cloaks & Outerwear

  • Fell and Fair. Linen ranger cloaks and sashes in rich colors. Pricey, but several Blades call it top tier.
  • Knightweave. Waterproof cloaks with good reviews. A $125 waterproof cloak went on sale in June 2026.
  • Medieval Collectibles. Cloaks that take a waterproofing spray well. Reapply each season.
  • WeDieWithStyle. Custom half cloaks and capes. A wool outer with a linen lining stays comfortable in heat.

Footwear

  • Fell and Fair ranger boots. One Blade has worn them for over a year, and walks in them all week after adding cushion soles. Check their sizing chart carefully.
  • Kult of Athena for hobnailed medieval-style shoes that are durable and easy to care for.
  • Hynafol community makers sell decorum boots at good prices. Ask in the Hynafol Discord.
  • Combat boots are fine for comfort, but modern soles are what get flagged for decorum. Tops that pass can still be undone by the sole.
  • Almost all historical footwear needs insoles to be comfortable. Gaiters cover modern laces and help in the heat.
  • Verillas sells shoes, but one Blade found them steep at about $349.

Garb & Tights

A basic Blade outfit that several Blades have worn: an affordable paneled shirt, tights or puffy pants, knee-high socks, and a big hat.

Gypsy Dream Studios ("Herman's Tights")

Thick cotton spandex tights, unisex, on Etsy. Base price was about $55 in 2026. The seller is communicative and does custom designs (more complex designs cost more), and Blades pair them with a puffy shirt and belt. Tights listing · Shop

"Where do I get Landsknecht trousers and hosen?"

  • Apparatus LARP (Etsy). Ready-made slashed and paneled pieces, with natural fabrics that dye easily. A lot of the House's ready-made Landsknecht clothing comes from here.
  • Atemporalia (Etsy). Doublets, shorts, hosen, and hats. The shorts have a reliable fit, and the House has negotiated 25 percent off group orders. Watch for periodic 25 percent sales.
  • Burgschneider. Offers a blue and gold Warhammer Empire line, with pre-orders when new batches are made.
  • Coeur de Mithril. Blue Lansquenet pants.
  • HEMAD (Etsy). Landsknecht Trousers "Morold" in blue and yellow, plus a matching lace-up shirt. Shipping can cost as much as the pants.
  • Leonardo Carbone. Lansquenet jackets and vests. The House orders from them.
  • Steel Mastery. Custom Waffenrock and pants. Expensive.
  • TheMedievals (Etsy). Wool Landsknecht shirts and hosen.
  • Verillas. Durable pants. They run sales, including end-of-season, and some Blades bought three pairs for the price of wool ones.
  • WeDieWithStyle. Fully customizable outfits and an online builder, with asymmetric options. Expensive, but Blades describe it as very durable and well made. They need lead time.

"Where do I get tights?"

  • Gypsy Dream Studios is the current answer (see the card above).
  • T'ger Toggs is a tights and pants maker with a physical shop at the Kansas City Renaissance Festival. It is the main alternative.
  • Seamlyne made popular multicolored tights and a functional codpiece, but has closed. Do not wait for a restock.

Codpieces and accessories

  • Copper Raven: belt mounts and aglets.
  • Custom leather codpiece (Etsy listing). A couple of Blades have bought from this seller.
  • Twiggs & Berries (Storenvy): royal and novelty codpieces.

Surcoats and tabards

Medieval Collectibles stocks tabards in blue and yellow, and a Blade used a custom Etsy maker for appliqué of the Golden Blades shield. Ask in the Discord for the current maker.

Hats & Headwear

Almost every Blade owns a big hat. Cheap ones are easy to find, so most Blades save their money for a statement piece.

  • ArcheoStudios (Etsy). Plume holders for helmets and flagpoles, and pilgrim badges and pins for hats.
  • Armstreet. Wool hats and linen clothing, with regular sales.
  • Atemporalia (Etsy). Hats alongside her doublets.
  • WeDieWithStyle (headgear). Hats, caps, and berets that a few Blades have bought. Expensive, but very good.

Maintenance

Once you own metal, keep it from rusting:

  • For bad rust, use a 500-grit sanding block.
  • Use WD-40 as a quick fix, and wipe down with a terry cloth.
  • Blades recommend Ballistol over WD-40 for long-term protection.
  • Renaissance wax seals and protects metal, but you have to reapply it often. Medieval Collectibles carries it, and cheaper options exist.
  • Some vendors store armor under a coat of mineral oil between events.

Weapons, Shields & Ranged

Read the weapons check notes first, and ask your Company before buying.

"What is the best pike?"

  • B3. Lighter pikes and glaives. One Blade finds it hard to recommend a zweihander from anywhere else over B3's.
  • Calimacil. The Fister pike is the heaviest of the options, and it comes in shorter lengths (down to 5 feet) with alternate heads. House orders get good prices.
  • Fakesteel. Pike and halberd heads that Blades like for building a polearm affordably. Check approval first.

"Where do I get a sword, mace, or dagger?"

  • Atelier Nemesis. Premium, "big bucks", usually approved. Also makes custom daggers.
  • Calimacil. The workhorse. Often approved, and slow to ship.
  • Epic Armoury. Cheaper, with mixed approval. Ask before you order, especially for a dagger like the stiletto where you want enough foam to be safe.

"Where do I get a shield?"

  • Epic Armoury. The Footman's shield has three adjustable inside straps, and the RFB buckler is a favorite. Some Blades are looking at the RFB kite shield as an arm-strapped shield for the pike wall. The Elven buckler is a minimal option that leaves a hand free.
  • Pike units are allowed one unbreakable shield, and many pikemen carry one strapped to the arm. Confirm with your Company.

"Where do I get a crossbow or bolts?"

  • LARP Distribution for the Bernardo crossbows (standard, pistol, and double).
  • B3 for bolts and an RFB crossbow.
  • Mytholon for crossbows through Medieval Collectibles.
  • Importing crossbows from Europe has been a customs headache. Buy through a US distributor.

Banners and flagpoles

Burgschneider has sold bunting flags. Epic Armoury sells flag poles, but they sell out, so watch for stock and batch a House order.
