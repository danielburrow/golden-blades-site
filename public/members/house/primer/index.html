Who We Are

The Golden Blades are the most professional mercenaries in the Known World of Hynafol, and we make sure everyone knows it.

We treat soldiering for hire as a business. Contracts are formal, paperwork matters, and breaking a contract is a serious wrong. Hunting for a favorable reading of the exact wording is simply good business. We are lively, good-humored, and slow to hold grudges. Our stated goals are earning coin and enjoying it, because mercenary work is dangerous and wealth is meant to be spent.

Anton von Forst, a veteran sellsword, formed the first version of the regiment in 807 A.C. to collect battlefield loot. After a treasure find and a lot of camaraderie it became the Golden Blades, and our first fortified office, the Palazzo in Port Nathair, soon followed. We are members of the Holy Reman League, our House's Kingdom.

Membership is voluntary. The Blades are a contractual enterprise, so you can negotiate your exit at any time. We punish desertion, sabotage, and spycraft, but we want you to find success the way you see fit. Your voice matters from day one, so share concerns and ideas about the direction and culture of the regiment.

Colors

Goldenrod and dark blue. If it is blue and gold, it is ours.

Look

Fifteenth and sixteenth century Italian condottieri, Swiss troops, and Holy Roman Empire Landsknechts. Flashy clothing, jewelry, sashes, and plumes.

Chain of command

Fighters, then officers, then the Oberst, then the employer. Blades take orders from our own command structure only.

The Regola Condotta

Every Blade agrees to the Regola when they sign on. Six rules:

  • Contratto (Contract). A mercenary follows contracts. If a contract is signed, it must be attempted faithfully. No exceptions.
  • Azienda (Company). A mercenary is loyal to their company. Without your company, you are dead. With your company, you are a threat.
  • Denaro (Coin). A mercenary fights for coin, not for ideals, nations, or people. A smart mercenary collects half up front.
  • Missione (The Job). A mercenary prioritizes the mission. When you are paid to complete a job, give it your full attention.
  • Rancori (Grudges). A mercenary conducts business on the battlefield, not score-settling. Resolve your grudges on your own time.
  • Sopravvivenza (Survival). A mercenary's legacy is survival. Defend your life and the lives of your comrades. We are not martyrs who exult in sacrifice.

Joining Up

You do not need to be a veteran. Plenty of Blades came to their first Hynafol event, met us, and signed on.

  1. Find a point of recruitment. One of our flyers or any officer will do. You will recognize them.
  2. Sign on as a contractor. Tell them you want to sign up as a contractor or sub-contractor, or to offer another skill to the Blades. You will be shown the Regola Condotta and given a contract.
  3. Work your first contracts. Side jobs alongside full members show everyone you know which end of the sword is dangerous.
  4. Ask for a full enlistment contract. It lasts until the next Grand Gathering, and you can re-enlist for the year.
  5. Face the Court of Pikes. With mutual agreement, an officer brings you before the Court and vouches for you. If there are no objections, you are sworn in under a hedge of pikes.

Choices to make as a new Blade

  • Your company. Pick one of the five Great Companies, but nothing is set in stone. A Sapper can carry a pike in the infantry block, and an Infantryman can write contracts. A company mostly gives you identity and flavor for your roleplay.
  • The Briefadel. Decide whether you want to be landed nobility. See Land & Nobility. Many Blades choose not to, and that is also fine.
  • The Internal Stock Market. Donating resources, units, or currency raises your voting power. It is optional.

What recruiters look for

  • Out-of-character conduct comes first. You can be goofy, but you act like an adult in chat and in person.
  • In-character compatibility. Would your character have a good time and plenty of roleplay with us?
  • Maturity. Can you separate LARP from the real world, and step back if bleed gets heavy?
  • Sense of fun. Can you enjoy not being the center of the storyline?
  • Vibes. Do you put the feeling of being a character, and everyone having a good time, above winning?

New members are encouraged to recruit friends, but give an honest recommendation and vibe-check them first. It benefits everyone.

Ranks & Roles

The Golden Blades run on a simple ranked hierarchy. This is our own chain of command, separate from the structure of any kingdom or league. See the Golden Blades hierarchy for who holds each seat.

OberstColonel, commander of the regiment
UnteroberstSecond in command, when numbers allow
Regimental StaffChief Adjutant, Intelligence, Human Resources, Logistics
HauptmannLeads each Great Company (the Marina's leader is the Kommandantin)
Section leadersRun the sections inside each company
BladesFighters, specialists, and recruits

Hauptmann titles

Each Hauptmann carries an ordinal title: Ersterhauptmann (Infantry), Zweiterhauptmann (Jaeger), Dreiterhauptmann (Condottiere), and Vierterhauptmann (Sapper). The Marina Mercantile is led by the Kommandantin, a rank equal to Hauptmann. An Ersterhauptmann or Zweiterhauptmann may also serve as Unteroberst.

How the structure scales

  • The Unteroberst (literally "Under Colonel") exists only if the regiment is large enough. With fewer than 30 people, the first company Hauptmann takes the temporary rank of Erster Hauptmann (First Captain) instead.
  • When a company grows past about 10 people, its section leaders take control of their sections to ease the load on the Hauptmann.

Pay, Contracts & Stock

We work for money. Clients bid for our soldiers on each battle, and every fighter gets a pay slip for it.

The basics

  • No house dues and no taxes. That is a standing promise.
  • The Contract Board lists skirmish contracts and open slots. Check it often. Paper copies come to Grand Gathering.
  • Pay slips. Make sure you get one after each fight, and cash it out. Tell the Paymaster or an officer if you fought and did not receive one.
  • Sub-contractors are paid under the same system, with slightly different terms.

The Regimental Operating Fee

The Golden Blades Regiment, LLC keeps part of large purses to fund non-combat personnel, grow the House, and cover pay for defensive battles and battles paid in non-monetary assets. Pay rates and the exact fee change regularly, so ask an officer for the current numbers before you negotiate. Two rules hold steady: a Blade's pay always exceeds the fee, and sub-contractor fees never exceed their pay.

The Internal Stock Market

Every Blade is a shareholder. Stock is tracked on stock cards, and it is how voting power is decided at Shareholder's Meetings and Courts of Pikes. Blades submit resources, citizen cards, currency, units, and building deeds, and receive stock in return.

  • Each Grand Gathering you attend as a Blade is noted on your stock card. Attendance also counts toward the pension paid to a Blade who leaves the organization.
  • Each citizen card you give earns a mark in its citizen category and unlocks your Briefadel resources for that event. Citizen card counts break ties at Shareholder's Meetings.

Contracts with clients

Contracts are written down and sealed. Before the work begins, either side may dissolve a contract by paying a penalty. If the Blade dissolves it, any up-front payment goes back with 50% added on top (rounded up). If the client dissolves it, any up-front payment is forfeit, and with no up-front payment the client owes 50% of the total.

Land & Nobility

The Briefadel, our "letter nobility," are the Blades who hold land. Choosing this path means listing buildings or settlements under your name. Over time it earns you a title such as Lord or Lady and a formal role in matters of state.

  • Landowners suggest new buildings, collect and share their hexes' resources, and pay the upkeep on them.
  • Lords and Ladies who control buildings pay their upkeep and collect their output from the Treasurer.
  • Higher nobles (Barons and Baronesses, Counts and Contessas, Dukes and Duchesses) decide what to build, set taxes, and maintain their hex holdings.
  • Collecting. The Treasurer collects the House's resources from the Counting House on the first day of an event. Nobles may collect their share until the first battle the next day, and anything uncollected after that is treated as donated to the House.
  • Upkeep must be paid to the Treasurer before Grand Gathering.
  • Briefadel pay. Nobles are paid in pay slips, each redeemable for currency, resources, or stock, and tradable.
  • A noble may leave the House, though it is frowned upon. They lose support for further expansion and must give up their holdings if they declare for another House.

You never have to be nobility to be a full Blade. For the full rules, see Briefadel and Ownership in the Carta Arcanum section.

Kit & Colors

There is no standard uniform and no dress code. Take maximum pride in your chosen appearance, and make it ostentatious.

  • Blue and gold (yellow). No shade restriction, because good dye is hard to find. Wear both colors where you can, and add one extra color for personal flair.
  • Think big. Voluminous fabrics, slashed sleeves, feathers, and ribbons. Almost every Blade also owns a large hat or other eye-catching headwear.
  • Weapons and armor depend on your company. See the table under The Great Companies, and ask a Blade in your company before you buy.
  • Need to borrow? Companies lend weapons and unit colors to new recruits when they can, from a regimental supply kept by the Quartermaster.
  • A gear question? Ask in the Blades Discord. Someone has almost certainly solved it already.

On the Field

  • Formation. The front rank repeats pike, halberd, shield. The rear ranks are pike, pike, and a halberd if one is available.
  • Present Arms is the moment to stop walking and step carefully, avoiding passing steps. Order Arms and Ready Arms save energy and should be used freely.
  • Pike basics. A pike is a two-handed, thrust-only spear between 85 and 108 inches long, with a red ring marked 20 inches from the base. Keep your hands on opposite sides of the ring, and never let your forward hand slide past it. An overhand grip steers thrusts low, away from heads and necks. Carry a sidearm for enemies inside your reach, and remember that a hit to your pike counts as a hit on the hand holding it.
  • Cadence beats courage. A pike block works only when it moves and fights as one body. Step past fallen comrades to keep the line, and finish the enemy in front of you before chasing an objective.
  • Cohesion, discipline, and confidence turn a pike line from serviceable to unstoppable. Show up to drills.
  • Hold your contract. If we are hired to guard a perimeter, we guard that perimeter and nothing more.
  • Stay hydrated. Texas heat is no joke, especially on contract days.

Meetings & Customs

Court of Pikes

Our assembly and induction ceremony. New Blades are sworn in under a hedge of pikes, and the Court meets in person at events, including at Grand Gathering, or virtually. Every Blade should experience one.

The Oberst's Salute

Our formal greeting: point the first two fingers of your right hand upward, then straighten your wrist and lay the hand over your left breast.

Shareholder's Meeting and Survey

Once a year we hold a Shareholder's Meeting, with a drill session and dinner when it is in person (see Annual Shareholders' Meeting). Beforehand, everyone fills out the anonymous Annual Shareholder Survey. It sets the agenda, so speak up.

Regimental events

The Oberst announces a regimental event at least half a day ahead. Turnout is strongly encouraged.

  • Court of Pikes. The companies assemble for key regimental business. Wear your more formal attire.
  • Regimental Gambling Hall. Everyone helps host card, dice, and other games for outsiders and Blades to raise funds for the regiment.
  • Regimental Muster. All companies assemble for announcements and inspection, usually at the start of the day. Wear the serving attire you will use that day.
  • Regimental Tournament. Each company sends champions to test skills across four events.
  • Shareholder Meeting. Economic decisions and minor business. Casual attire, and questionable sobriety is expected.

Companies are also encouraged to host their own events: fencing practice with the Infantry, an archery contest with the Jaeger, an artillery expo with the Sappers, healing, potion making and calligraphy with the Condottiere, and mapmaking with the Marina.

Trade and merchants

Blades run storefronts, the Night of Fortune, and merchant stalls. Marina Mercantile organizes trade routes and ships, so ask if you want to help.

Roles

Hynafol asks players to hold only one major role (guild leader, secretary, treasurer, or a religion's high priest, scribe, or custodian). Spread the responsibility and the fun around.

Awards

Awards are given by the current Oberst, and possibly by a former Oberst, for great deeds on behalf of the regiment. A Hauptmann nominates, and Blades cannot nominate themselves. Each award reflects what the nominating officer saw. Repeat awards are marked with a number on the first ribbon you received. At an award ceremony, officers describe each deed before the regiment, and the Oberst (current or former) pins on the awards.

Ceremonies

Joining

Hauptmanns bring recruits to the Oberst at a Court of Pikes. Each recruit walks under an arch of pikes made by the existing members, so the Oberst can look over their armament and fitness. The Hauptmann vouches for them and the Oberst gives verbal approval. The recruit then reads their Contract of Service aloud to the regiment, signs it, and goes to their company for training.

Promotion

Promotions are decided inside the company by the Hauptmann, based on skill in the section and leadership ability. Only the Oberst promotes Hauptmanns and above. Everyone the new rank will command attends, and the presiding officer administers an oath: loyalty to the regiment, responsibility to the new rank and the mercenaries under it, and acceptance of the position. The new officer then receives the sigil or crest of their command.

Retirement

A Blade who has served faithfully can officially retire only at the end of their last day, at a convened Court of Pikes. The regiment forms an arch of pikes and the retiree is presented to the Oberst for a ceremonial last inspection, with their deeds vouched for by their officers. The Oberst declares them "fit to retire and live freely," marks their contract "served and paid" beneath the original seal, and hands over their last payment.

The Kinderguart

The Kinderguart is the section for young mercenaries who are too young to take part fully in Hynafol events. Their missions scale to their age, and every mercenary keeps a ledger book that officials stamp.

  • Diplomacy. Visit every kingdom and ask if its leader needs anything from the Blades, collecting a stamp from each.
  • Fort Architect. Design a Palazzo in your ledger and explain it to a Sapper for approval.
  • Messenger Service. Carry messages or small packages for the regiment or its employers, with a signature or stamp from each recipient.
  • Seal Making. Draw and help carve a personal stamp, then stamp it on the cover of your new ledger. This is the first mission.
  • Survey the Land. Update the Blades' maps by drawing what you see at four guild camps or landmarks.

Pay is in Gelt (chocolate coins), or Tootsie Rolls for kids with allergies.

Vibes

We are the peak of sportsmanship, vibes, and good times. In practice:

  • Keep the rivalries in character. Plenty of us have friends in the factions we fight.
  • Questions about politics, feuds, or the Imperium? Ask the Oberst directly and not in group chat. Group chat turns specifics into a chorus.
  • Altruism is for the poor. Get the bag, but be generous with the people beside you.
  • Commit to the bit. Our best moments are ridiculous ones.
